# Inventory Management Standard Operating Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how the distribution company monitors inventory, prevents stockouts, allocates scarce product, and coordinates replenishment. It establishes consistent ownership and escalation across all warehouses and product categories.

## 2. Scope and inventory ownership

This procedure applies to all company-managed stock, including saleable, reserved, damaged, quarantined, and in-transit quantities. The inventory system of record is authoritative for book balances; physical counts and receiving records are used to investigate and correct discrepancies.

- **Warehouse Operations** owns physical custody, timely transaction entry, count accuracy, and local inventory review.
- **Inventory Planning** owns safety-stock settings, replenishment signals, demand and supply review, and shortage coordination.
- **Purchasing** owns supplier commitments, purchase-order follow-up, and alternate-source requests.
- **Customer-Facing Teams** own customer communication and account-specific service requirements.

Inventory adjustments require a reason code, supporting evidence, timestamp, and approval under the company’s financial-control limits.

## 3. Safety-stock monitoring and low-stock thresholds

Safety stock is the approved minimum buffer intended to protect service against demand and supply variability. Available inventory is on-hand quantity less allocated quantity; in-transit supply must be tracked separately and must not be counted as received stock.

Use these escalation thresholds for each product and warehouse:

| Condition | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| Available quantity is at or above safety stock | Continue normal monitoring and replenishment planning. |
| Available quantity is below safety stock | Review demand, allocations, open replenishment, and projected days of supply. |
| Below safety stock for more than **3 consecutive business days** | Inventory Planning must complete and document a shortage review with Warehouse Operations and Purchasing. |
| Available quantity is below **50% of safety stock** | Escalate immediately to the Inventory Planning lead and Warehouse Manager; begin stockout-risk coordination. |
| Current or projected stockout threatens a service commitment | Notify Customer-Facing Teams promptly and follow the customer communication procedure. |

Business days exclude weekends and company-observed holidays. Consecutive-day monitoring resumes after any business day on which available inventory returns to or above safety stock.

## 4. Daily inventory review

Warehouse Operations and Inventory Planning review inventory each business day, preferably before the first outbound wave:

1. Review on-hand, allocated, available, quarantined, and in-transit quantities for active products.
2. Identify items below safety stock, below 50% of safety stock, at risk of stockout, or showing unusual demand or inventory movement.
3. Check open orders, reservations, unposted receipts, transfer activity, and recent adjustments for accuracy.
4. Compare the latest position with forecast demand, open customer commitments, replenishment dates, and days of supply.
5. Assign an owner and due date to each exception; record actions in the inventory or planning system.

Warehouse Operations immediately records receipts, picks, transfers, damages, and count corrections so that the review uses current transactions.

## 5. Stockout escalation and replenishment

When a shortage threshold is met, Inventory Planning opens an exception record with product, warehouse, affected quantity, current availability, safety-stock target, first date below threshold, projected stockout date, and potentially affected commitments.

Purchasing must review **all open purchase orders for affected SKUs**, confirm supplier acknowledgment, quantities, expected delivery dates, shipment status, and any receiving constraints. Purchasing requests an updated committed date and recovery options when supply is late or insufficient. Inventory Planning evaluates inter-warehouse transfers, approved substitutes, expedited supply, and revised replenishment quantities using cost and service impact.

Replenishment quantities must follow approved planning parameters and account for open orders, in-transit receipts, forecast demand, minimum order quantities, and storage constraints. Changes to safety stock or planning parameters require documented analysis and approval by the designated planning authority.

## 6. Allocation rules during shortages

When available inventory cannot cover all demand, Inventory Planning coordinates a documented allocation decision with Warehouse Operations and Customer-Facing Teams:

1. Protect safety, regulatory, and contractual obligations.
2. Prioritize orders by committed delivery date and approved customer priority.
3. Allocate remaining supply fairly across comparable customers; do not reserve stock based only on order size or internal preference.
4. Consider partial fulfillment only when operationally feasible, customer terms permit it, and the customer communication plan is agreed.
5. Record quantities allocated, deferred, substituted, or transferred, with the decision owner and review time.

High-priority customers receive the service specified in their contract or approved account plan. Priority status does not remove the need to document allocation decisions or assess impact on other commitments.

## 7. Warehouse and purchasing responsibilities

Warehouse Managers maintain count discipline, investigate location and transaction errors, protect reserved stock, and report capacity or receiving constraints. Inventory Planners monitor thresholds, validate demand signals, coordinate allocations, and maintain exception records. Purchasing maintains supplier confirmations, escalates delivery risk, and records revised commitments against the purchase order.

## 8. Exception management and closure

An exception remains open until inventory is replenished, a transfer or substitute is approved, customer commitments are resolved, or an authorized disposition is recorded. The owner updates status at least once per business day while customer service is at risk. Closure notes include the cause category, quantity and dates affected, actions taken, approvals, customer-team notification, and any parameter or process changes required to prevent recurrence.
