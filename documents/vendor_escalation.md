# Vendor Performance and Escalation Procedure

## 1. Purpose and scope

This procedure establishes how Purchasing and Supply Chain monitor vendor commitments, respond to late deliveries, and address repeated performance failures. It applies to purchase orders for inventory, packaging, and other supply that can affect warehouse operations or customer commitments.

## 2. Vendor performance expectations

Vendors are expected to acknowledge purchase orders, confirm quantities and delivery dates, meet agreed specifications, provide accurate shipment information, and communicate risks before a commitment is missed. Purchase-order terms and written vendor commitments are the source for expected delivery dates and quantities.

Purchasing reviews vendor on-time delivery, lead-time reliability, quantity fulfillment, quality issues, responsiveness, and open corrective actions. Performance is assessed over a consistent reporting period and by product or site when the data supports that level of detail.

## 3. Purchase-order monitoring

Purchasing owns the vendor relationship and purchase-order follow-up. For each open order, the buyer:

1. Confirms vendor acknowledgment, ordered quantity, ship method, and expected delivery date.
2. Reviews open orders at least weekly and checks due or at-risk orders each business day.
3. Requests shipment confirmation and tracking details when the planned ship date approaches.
4. Compares actual receipt date and quantity with the purchase-order commitment.
5. Records revised commitments, vendor contacts, affected items and receiving locations, and next actions in the purchasing system.

Expected delivery dates must not be changed solely to make a late order appear on time. Any revision requires a documented vendor update, buyer rationale, and an audit trail that preserves the original commitment.

## 4. Late-delivery thresholds and escalation

Lateness is measured in **calendar days after the confirmed expected delivery date**. For a shipment with multiple lines or partial receipts, assess the undelivered balance against its own committed date. Apply the highest threshold reached and maintain the incident until recovery is verified.

| Days late | Escalation level | Required action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1–2 days** | Monitor and contact vendor | Buyer contacts the vendor, confirms shipment status and revised arrival date, and records the response. Check customer or production exposure. |
| **3–5 days** | Formal Purchasing escalation | Purchasing lead reviews the miss, contacts the vendor’s account owner, requests a written recovery commitment, and assesses alternate supply or transfer options. |
| **6–10 days** | Supply Chain leadership escalation | Supply Chain leadership assigns an incident owner and requires a dated recovery plan with shipment milestones, quantity by milestone, and daily updates until stabilized. |
| **More than 10 days** | Critical supplier incident | Notify the executive sponsor promptly. Complete a customer-impact assessment, establish a mitigation plan, and communicate material risk to affected internal teams. |

If a delay threatens a customer commitment before its day-based threshold is reached, escalate immediately to the level needed to protect service.

## 5. Supplier communication and recovery plans

Vendor communications should identify the purchase order and line, product, quantity outstanding, original expected date, days late, receiving location, customer or operational exposure, and the decision or commitment requested. Ask the vendor to state the reason for delay, current shipment location, confirmed recovery date, quantity available, and next update time.

A recovery plan must name accountable contacts and include dated actions, shipment or production milestones, quantities, transport arrangements, and contingency options. Purchasing records all commitments and checks progress against each milestone. Verbal commitments are summarized in writing and retained with the purchase-order record.

## 6. Corrective-action review and repeated failures

Vendors with repeated late deliveries enter corrective-action review. Repeated failure includes **three or more late purchase orders in a rolling 90-day period**, or **two consecutive late deliveries** for a critical product. Purchasing and Supply Chain leadership review root-cause evidence, recurrence, customer exposure, recovery performance, and whether sourcing or safety-stock controls need adjustment.

The vendor submits a corrective-action plan with cause analysis, containment, corrective steps, accountable owners, due dates, and measurable effectiveness checks. Purchasing tracks actions to completion and escalates missed actions or another late delivery to Supply Chain leadership. Continued failure may result in a sourcing review or limits on new awards, subject to contract and procurement authority.

## 7. Executive escalation and incident closure

Executive notification is required for a delay greater than 10 days, a critical supply interruption, material customer exposure, or a mitigation decision beyond delegated authority. The incident owner provides a concise status covering affected orders and locations, customer impact, supply gap, recovery milestones, decisions needed, and next update time.

Close an escalation only after the outstanding quantity is received or an approved mitigation is in place, customer exposure is addressed, vendor commitments are recorded, and required corrective actions have owners and due dates. Retain the timeline and final disposition for vendor scorecards and future sourcing decisions.
