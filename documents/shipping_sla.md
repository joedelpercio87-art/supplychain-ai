# Customer Shipping and Service Level Agreement Procedure

## 1. Purpose and scope

This procedure defines how the distribution company measures delivery service, manages shipping exceptions, and communicates service risk to customers. It applies to customer orders shipped from company warehouses unless a customer contract specifies a stricter process or target.

## 2. OTIF, on-time, and in-full definitions

- **On time:** The complete order is delivered on or before the requested delivery date recorded for that order. A carrier scan is evidence of progress; the delivery confirmation is the evidence of delivery.
- **In full:** The customer receives the full quantity ordered across all order lines, after approved substitutions or cancellations are accounted for under the customer’s terms.
- **OTIF:** An order is OTIF only when it is delivered on or before its requested delivery date **and** the full quantity ordered is delivered. An order that is late or short is not OTIF.

OTIF is measured at the order level. Report both the numerator (orders delivered on time and in full) and denominator (eligible orders) so that the rate is interpretable. Customer-specific exclusions or delivery rules must be documented before reporting and applied consistently.

## 3. Customer service targets and review thresholds

The company-wide baseline target is **at least 95% OTIF** for each reporting period. A customer contract or approved account plan takes precedence when it sets a stricter target. Priority accounts must receive the target and response cadence recorded in their account plan.

| Measured OTIF | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| **95% or higher** | Continue routine monitoring and service reporting. |
| **Below 95%** | Operations conducts a review of late and incomplete orders, identifies affected processes, and assigns corrective actions. |
| **Below 90%** | Escalate to the Operations Manager and Customer Service lead; set an owner and review cadence. |
| **Below 80%** | Open a formal corrective-action review with accountable leaders, milestones, and effectiveness measures. |

Apply the thresholds to the agreed reporting period and customer or service scope. A severe individual incident or high-risk commitment may require escalation even when the aggregate rate remains above target.

## 4. Warehouse shipping responsibilities

Warehouse Operations validates order, item, and destination details before release; protects allocated stock; picks and checks quantities; applies required labels and documentation; and tenders shipments by the committed ship date. Staff record dispatch, carrier, tracking reference, quantities shipped, and exception status in the system of record. Receiving or carrier events that indicate delay or damage must be recorded promptly.

Warehouse Managers review missed dispatches, short picks, mis-shipments, and held orders each business day. They coordinate with Inventory Planning on shortages and with Transportation on carrier or capacity issues. Shipment dates and quantities must reflect actual events; corrections require a reason and audit trail.

## 5. Partial and late shipments

A partial shipment is any order shipped or delivered with less than the full ordered quantity. It does not meet the in-full definition and therefore cannot count as OTIF, even if the shipped portion arrives on time. Split shipments require approval under customer terms, clear remaining-quantity tracking, and an agreed plan for the balance.

A shipment is late when delivery occurs after the requested delivery date. When a dispatch or tracking milestone makes a miss likely, Transportation and Warehouse Operations verify the latest status, revised arrival estimate, quantity affected, and recovery options. Record the reason category without changing the original requested date.

## 6. Customer notification and priority handling

Customer-Facing Teams must notify affected customers proactively when a shortage, late dispatch, carrier exception, or partial shipment threatens a service commitment. Communicate as soon as the risk is confirmed and, where practical, before the promised delivery date. Include the affected order and items, quantity at risk, current best delivery estimate, available alternatives, and the time of the next update. Do not promise an unconfirmed recovery date.

Priority customers receive the notification timing, escalation contact, and service recovery specified in their contract or approved account plan. Customer priority guides response urgency but does not authorize unapproved allocation or concealment of other customer impacts.

## 7. Service recovery

The case owner coordinates an appropriate remedy under customer terms: expedite the delayed balance, arrange an approved split shipment, transfer available inventory, offer an approved substitute, or agree a revised delivery commitment. Confirm cost and operational approval before arranging expedited transport or commercial concessions. Track the remedy through confirmed delivery and close the customer case only after the customer-facing outcome is recorded.

## 8. Root-cause review and corrective action

Operations reviews OTIF below the applicable threshold and significant individual incidents. The review compares order promises with actual dispatch, delivery, and shipped quantities; identifies contributing warehouse, inventory, carrier, or planning factors; and separates verified facts from hypotheses. Assign corrective actions with owners, due dates, and measurable checks. Communicate material findings and recovery progress to affected customers and management, and verify that actions improve later service results.
